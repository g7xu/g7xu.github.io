**Last updated**: 2026-07-06

## What versus How

The #1 failure mode: specifying the solution instead of the problem.

- Many solutions fit one problem — naming a solution hides the real requirement (10 customers ask for 10 features; root problem may just be *performance*)
- Predetermined solutions shut the door on better ones / new tech
- It undermines engineers — the solution is *their* contribution; recipes make them bristle
- Check per requirement: problem stated, or solution?

## Detail calibration

- **Too little** → team assumes ≠ things; something "not required" actually is. Fix: eng reviews early drafts
- **Too much** → nobody reads it, or obsolete before finished

## Less is more

- Features add complexity, support + maintenance cost; **fewer features → product perceived as MORE powerful** (users actually discover & use them)
- Barksdale: "keep the main thing the main thing"

## Who drives requirements (two bad extremes)

- **Engineering-driven** — built around exciting tech or a hard problem; PM must own "a product customers will buy"
- **Customer/sales-driven** — sales negotiates requirements; Hewlett: "marketing people must play a secondary role in product definition"

## Specials

- Special = custom version/feature promised to one customer for a deal
- Slippery slope: feature often isn't the real need, burdens everyone else, multiple versions bog down build/test/maintain
- Be willing to walk away; route true customization to system integrators

## Pay your taxes

- Big-bang re-architecture ("house of cards") kills products — customers see nothing shipping for months
- Prevention: reserve **~10–20% of every release** for foundation work, off the top, engineering's to spend

## You are *not* your customer

- HP "next-bench" worked only because HP sold to engineers
- Valley preferred ReplayTV (control/expandability) → market chose TiVo (simplicity); pundits hated AOL mail → mass market didn't care
- Reality-check with the actual target market, constantly

## Smaller ones

- **Usability testing too little/late** — test during requirements, multiple rounds, PM+designer present; watch what users *do*, not say
- **Emotion can be logical** — cutting every non-essential feature → dull product; know which features create the essential *emotional* response, treat as critical
- **Standard is better than better** — gratuitous differences (rental car's hidden door locks) distract from real innovation; innovate only where there's real need
