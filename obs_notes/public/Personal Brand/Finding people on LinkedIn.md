## Define your target first

Before sending a single request, define your ideal connection: industry, seniority, company size, geography, and any behavioral filters. Be specific — "SaaS founders in the EU who posted in the last 30 days" is a real target; "marketing professionals" is a category. The more specific the target, the more relevant the outreach, the higher the reply rate.

## The alumni filter (Jeff Su)

Go to your school's LinkedIn page → **Alumni** → use the search field three ways:

1. **By aspiration role** — you know the field you want and want someone more experienced to learn from. Want sales? Type "account manager" to list alumni with that title now or previously.
2. **By target company + role** — you want to learn about a specific position/company. Applying to marketing at Nike? Type "Nike, marketing" to see alumni who work or worked there.
3. **By inspiration** — expand the "Where they work" section to see which companies have the most alumni from your school.

**Pro tip:** the same filter works on any **company page → People** tab, for even more relevant connections.

## The Groups loophole (Jeff Su)

You can normally only message 1st-degree connections (or with Premium). But you *can* message anyone in a **LinkedIn Group you're both in**, without connecting first. Join your school's official group, alumni groups, and interest/industry groups where you share genuine background with members. Then open a group member's profile and message directly.

You can also **post a specific question** in a group ("interested in business-continuity consulting — what classes prepare me?") — niche questions can pull multiple helpful replies from alumni within a day, and following up expands your network and surfaces opportunities.

## Sales Navigator (paid, for scale)

Adds signal-based filters the free plan lacks: recent job changes, company headcount growth, and content engagement. Also lets you filter **group members** by title, company size, seniority, and geography — turning a broad industry group into a precise prospecting list. Worth the cost if outbound is a real part of your workflow; the free plan hits search limits quickly.

## Signal-based targeting (warm entry points)

Instead of cold lists, reach out to people who already signaled interest — this is the core shift described in [[LinkedIn Networking]]:

- **Post engagers** — anyone who liked/commented/shared your post has already raised their hand. Far warmer than a cold search result.
- **Profile viewers** — they already looked you up.
- **Event attendees** — shared attendance is a non-spammy reason to connect. After registering for a LinkedIn Event you can view the **Attendees** list, a pre-filtered, intent-signaling audience. Referencing the event ("I noticed you're attending [Event] — also following the [topic] space closely") makes the request land.
- **Group members** — a self-selected audience around a topic; use group membership as the context.

Once you've found the right people, connect with genuine context ([[LinkedIn connection requests]]) and warm them up first ([[LinkedIn follow-up and engagement]]).
