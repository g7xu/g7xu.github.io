## The follow-up sequence

Expandi's analysis of 70,000+ campaigns: the **second follow-up drives a +4.05% lift in reply rates**, yet most people stop after one message. (The first follow-up shows a slight ~0.6% dip, which is why so many give up too early — push past it.)

A practical cadence:

- **Day 1** — connection request with a personalized note ([[LinkedIn connection requests]]).
- **Day 3 (after acceptance)** — value-add first message. Share a relevant insight, ask a genuine question, or reference something specific from their work. Do **not** pitch.
- **Day 7** — soft follow-up if no reply. One low-friction sentence. Don't repeat the previous message.
- **Day 14** — final message. Make it easy to say no or come back later.

**The cardinal rule:** each touchpoint needs a *new angle* — a different piece of content, a fresh observation, a specific question. Repeating yourself signals you have no real reason to reach out and risks getting marked as spam. If you have nothing new to say, wait until you do.

## Social warming (before you connect)

Like or comment meaningfully on 2–3 of someone's posts *before* sending the request, then reference that in your note. This shifts you from "stranger who wants something" to "peer who pays attention." Manual at small scale; automatable at larger scale (with the caveats below).

## Staying visible

- **Comment quality > post volume.** One insightful comment on a target's post generates more relevant connection requests than three average posts of your own. Prioritize depth.
- **Post consistently.** Pick 2–3 topics at the intersection of what you know and what your audience cares about; post 2–3×/week; engage with comments the same day. Inbound connections are warmer than outbound. (This is also the bridge to the wider [[Personal Brand Overview]] content work.)
- **Acknowledge support.** Thank people who share your post, endorse you, or introduce you. Small gestures make people more likely to keep helping you.
- **Turn engagement into outreach.** Every like/comment on your content is a warm signal — follow up rather than treating it as a vanity metric.

## A weekly baseline

Networking is a long game. One suggested rhythm: ~3–5 posts, ~10 meaningful comments, ~20 targeted connection requests per week, reviewed at 30 and 60 days — not after one week. Consistency compounds.

## The automation tension (sources disagree)

- **Sales-tool sources (Expandi, Snov.io)** advocate automating the repeatable parts — connection requests, profile views, follow-up timing, endorsements, post-engagement triggers — while keeping targeting and messaging human. They rely on safety features (dedicated IPs, warm-up, daily limits) to stay within LinkedIn's thresholds.
- **Business.com warns** that automation carries platform and reputation risk: LinkedIn monitors automated activity and may restrict or suspend accounts, and generic mass messages read as spammy from the receiving end.

**Resolution to keep in mind:** automation only multiplies results if the underlying manual strategy already works — otherwise it just sends bad messages faster. For a personal brand / job search (vs. an SDR team at scale), the reputational downside of looking automated is higher, so lean manual and personal.
