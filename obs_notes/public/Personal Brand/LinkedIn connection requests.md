## The note debate (the surprising data)

Common belief: adding a note lifts your acceptance rate. The **Belkins 2025 study** found otherwise — acceptance rates are virtually identical with or without a note (**26.42% vs 26.37%**).

What the note *does* do is lift your **reply rate after connecting**: **9.36% with a note vs 5.44% without** — a ~71% improvement in actual conversations started.

**Takeaway:** don't write the note to get accepted. Write it to set up the first conversation. (This is also why you should never waste the note on a pitch — see below.)

## How to write the note

- **Keep it under ~100 characters.** LinkedIn caps connection notes; short and specific beats long.
- **Give one specific reason to connect** — a shared group, a post they published, a mutual contact, the same school/event, or a challenge their role likely faces.
- **Skip the pitch entirely.** Save any ask for after they accept ([[LinkedIn follow-up and engagement]]).
- **On mobile, use "More → Personalize invite"** — never the default one-tap Connect button, which sends no note.

## Jeff Su's three message types

Whether you found the person through search or are messaging them directly, a strong request does at least one of:

1. **Relatable** — connect your situation/background to theirs.
2. **Compliment** — reference something specific they made or did (a video, a post, a project). Specificity proves you actually paid attention.
3. **Add value** — offer a useful thought, not just a request.

Example he received (works because it's specific): *"Hi Jeff, came across your Q&A video on YouTube — really enjoyed the part about moving from management consulting into EdTech. I'm an Education student exploring consulting. Would love to stay connected."*

## Templates

**Shared context / role (Expandi):**
> "Hi [Name]. Saw you're scaling an SDR team at [Company]. Wanted to connect as I work with similar teams at this stage."

**Mutual connection (Snov.io):**
> "Hey [Name], our mutual connection Natalie mentioned your work in AI-driven marketing. I'm exploring similar strategies and thought it'd be great to connect and share perspectives."

**Shared event (Snov.io):**
> "Hey [Name]! We both attended the 'Sales Workflow Automation' web conference last week. Let's connect and exchange ideas!"

**Admiration / journey (Snov.io):**
> "Hey [Name]! I find your finance-to-tech journey inspiring. I'd love to connect and learn from your experience."

## Anti-patterns

- Generic "I'd like to add you to my professional network." / "Came across your profile and decided to connect."
- Pitching in the first message after they accept. Lead with what *they'd* find relevant.
- Mass-sending the same note to an unsegmented list. See [[Finding people on LinkedIn]] to target properly first.
