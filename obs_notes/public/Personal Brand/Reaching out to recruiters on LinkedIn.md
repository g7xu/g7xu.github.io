Part of the [[LinkedIn Networking]] cluster. Recruiters have outsized influence over the process and are bombarded daily — the default connection request won't make you stand out.

## The tip

End your message to the recruiter with a request to be **connected with someone currently working the role you're applying for.**

## Why it works

- **Recruiters help those who help themselves.** The ask shows you're proactive and doing the right things.
- **It signals problem-solving.** As an outside candidate you can't judge fit yet — but you know the fastest way to find out is to talk to someone doing the job. That reasoning is a transferable skill.
- **It's polite and considerate.** It's a more meaningful, specific request than "can I get 30 minutes to pick your brain?", so it stands out.

**Side benefit (independent of outreach):** always try to talk to someone in the role you're applying for. It gives you an edge at interview time *and* tells you whether the day-to-day is actually a good fit — before you commit.

## The three-step process

**Step 1 — Connect first (don't mention the job).** Use a warm, personalized note (relatable / compliment / add value — see [[LinkedIn connection requests]]). Goal is just to get accepted and start rapport.
> "Hi Annie, I see we're both at MNC tech companies based in Shanghai. I also noticed you did your bachelor's at UNNC, where our company recently ran a college outreach program. Would love to connect if possible."

**Step 2 — Ask for the introduction.** Now that you're connected, you can send a slightly longer message without seeming aggressive. Relate your background to the role and show consideration for their time.
> "Hi Annie, thanks for accepting. I saw the product marketing manager posting from two weeks ago and I'm researching the role to see if I'd be a good fit. Would you happen to know anyone on the team I could have a casual chat with? As a product marketer myself, I'd love to understand the day-to-day and team culture before bothering you with specifics on the application process."

**Step 3 — Follow up after the chat.** Close the loop to stay top of mind and reinforce your interest. Reference something specific from the conversation, then ask to send your resume as the next step.
> "Hi Annie, wanted to close the loop — my chat with Jane went really well, thank you for the intro. I had no idea the iOS App Store was creating so many opportunities for Greater China developers while staying within Apple's privacy policies. It's only solidified my interest, and I'd love to send my resume for your review. I genuinely think the stakeholder-management skills I built at Google would transfer well to this role."

## If the recruiter says no

Even a declined intro usually produces a better outcome than a cold request:
- "I can answer questions about the role myself" → you just got time with the recruiter.
- "Send your resume and I'll forward it to the hiring manager" → your resume gets forwarded instead of lost in the system.

All of these beat hoping a default connection request stands out.
