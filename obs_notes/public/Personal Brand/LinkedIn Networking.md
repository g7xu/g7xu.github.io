## The core shift: volume → signal

The default LinkedIn playbook (scrape a list, blast connection requests, pitch on accept) has the worst outcomes on the platform. The approach that works in 2026 is **signal-based**: reach out to people who already have a reason to know who you are — they viewed your profile, engaged with your content, attended the same event, or share your school/group. The signal does the qualification work, so the first message isn't cold.

## Principles all five sources agree on

- **Quality over quantity.** Connect with people who match your goals, not everyone. A large but irrelevant network tanks your engagement and dilutes your reach — LinkedIn surfaces your content to your connections.
- **Optimize your profile *before* you reach out.** It's the first thing anyone checks after your request. See [[LinkedIn profile optimization]].
- **Personalize with a specific reason.** Generic "I'd like to add you to my network" underperforms. See [[LinkedIn connection requests]].
- **Never pitch in the first message.** After someone accepts, start a conversation or add value; save any ask for touch 2 or 3.
- **Follow up — with a new angle each time.** Most outreach fails because there's no second message. See [[LinkedIn follow-up and engagement]].
- **Play the long game.** The strongest networks are built over months of consistent, small interactions, not a single campaign sprint.
