Part of the [[LinkedIn Networking]] cluster. If your profile looks incomplete, reads like a job description, or has no clear positioning, your acceptance rate drops before you've said a word. Fix these first.

## Highest-weight elements

**Headline.** Describe *who you help and what you do*, not just your job title. "Head of Growth at X" tells no one anything; "Helping B2B SaaS teams build outbound pipeline on LinkedIn" gives someone a reason to accept. Include keywords people would search for — the headline sits next to your name everywhere on the platform.

**Banner / background image.** A second chance to state your value proposition. Can carry a tagline or branded visual. Most people leave it blank; a concise value statement here stands out.

**About / summary.** Answer one question: *why should someone in your target audience want to connect with you?* Keep it to 3–4 first-person sentences. Tell a compact story of who you are, what you do, and the value or outcomes you create. Skip generic claims; name specific problems you solve. Include relevant keywords for search.

## Supporting elements

- **Profile photo.** Profiles with clear, professional headshots get more views and engagement. You don't need a studio — good lighting, simple background, appropriate attire.
- **Skills (≥5).** LinkedIn's algorithm heavily weights the Skills section when matching profiles in search. Add at least five that reflect your expertise and goals. See [[Finding people on LinkedIn]] for the search side of this.
- **Experience with achievements.** Focus on quantifiable results, not job duties — "increased sales by 20%", "led a team of 15 to launch new software". Short, specific, measurable beats long paragraphs.
- **Current title, location, education.** Location-based searches are common; a current location and title increase searchability.
- **Recommendations & endorsements.** Reinforce credibility and give new connections confidence — endorse others first, and they often reciprocate.

## Why it comes first

Your profile passively receives traffic the moment someone gets your request or sees your comment. Optimizing it is the cheapest lift to your acceptance and reply rates — it works while you sleep. Do it before running any of the outreach in [[LinkedIn connection requests]] or [[Reaching out to recruiters on LinkedIn]].
