The conceptual anchor for the [[LinkedIn Networking]] cluster and the wider [[Personal Brand Overview]] domain.

## The idea

Mark Granovetter (Stanford) found that in his study **84% of respondents got their job through a "weak" relationship** — an acquaintance, not a close friend.

The mechanism: people you spend a lot of time with **swim in the same pool of information** you do. They know what you already know. So you depend on *friendly outsiders* — weak ties — to bring you information and opportunities from beyond your immediate circle.

Jeff Su describes this from experience: a friend who's now close, but was "an acquaintance at best" at the time, is the one who helped him land a job at Google.

## Why it matters for LinkedIn

LinkedIn is, in effect, a tool for finding and activating weak ties at scale — alumni, former colleagues, people in a target company or role, group members. That reframes the whole cluster:

- It's **not** about deepening relationships with people you already know well.
- It **is** about purposefully reaching people one or two steps outside your circle who can bridge you to new information and opportunities — see [[Finding people on LinkedIn]].
- It's why a genuine, specific connection request to a near-stranger ([[LinkedIn connection requests]]) is worth the effort, and why the [[Reaching out to recruiters on LinkedIn|recruiter method]] — asking to be introduced to someone in the role — is so effective: you're deliberately building a bridge to a new pool of information.

## Pay it forward

The norm cuts both ways: when someone reaches out to *you* as their weak tie, help them. A healthy network of weak ties depends on reciprocity.
